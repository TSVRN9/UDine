UDine Flow Lab

Four alternative flows for the same app, built as touch prototypes on real UMass menus for Thursday, Oct 8. Open each on your phone and judge the feel.

Benchmark: at Worcester lunch, log Beef Brisket Noodle Bowl ×1 and Golden BBQ Chicken Thigh ×2. The current app takes about 5 taps plus scrolling past roughly 20 dishes.
M2

M1, rebuilt on App Store and Material motion

Same flow as M1, on platform navigation. Hall bands and pinned cafés grow into their page; everything else pushes in from the right, with an edge swipe back. A dish opens as a sheet over the list you were in, saying where you can get it now or the next meal it's served. Cafés share the halls' look: maroon photo bands when pinned, and the same page when opened. Turn on healthfulness and carbon grade scores for every dish from You. Events are banner cards on Search. Tabs fade through; meal tabs slide on a shared axis. Round 9: pick any day through Oct 22 from the day control beside the meals; tap it or the open-until line for the hall's schedule, address and directions. Cafés now have real dishes with prices, and Search finds them. Avoid allergens in Dietary preferences. Add ?net=slow, offline or fail to the address to see the connection states.

M1

Hall-first, search-forward, varsity

The merged flow from your feedback. Halls, Search and You tabs. Each hall band carries its real photo under the chenille letter; opening a hall develops the photo into the menu header, which collapses into a maroon bar as you scroll. Search covers every hall with lenses, keeps the keyboard up between picks, and adds straight to the plate. The station header opens a station list. Plate totals read like a Nutrition Facts panel. Later rounds added real hours, 27 cafés grouped by area, a busy meter, events in the Search tab, full Nutrition Facts with ingredients and grades, a station switcher, and feedback on every interaction.

Benchmark
5 taps, no scrolling via Search; 5 taps plus about two flicks via the hall
Watch for
The photo transition, whether the station list beats scrolling, and Log staying visible above the keyboard on iPhone. Busy values are simulated for lunch. Photos are UMass Dining's, shown unmodified as in the official app.

F1

Dish-first, right now

Opens on everything being served this meal across all four halls, grouped by kind of food. Halls are a filter instead of the entry point. The plate's hall is whichever hall serves everything on it, so you never pick a hall up front. A dish from another hall starts a second plate you can swipe to compare.

Benchmark
4 taps, no scrolling
Watch for
The list regathers around your hall after the first add; ranking decides what's visible.
Open F1
F2

Habits and after-the-meal logging

Home is you today: totals so far, past plates that are being served again ("Same as Tuesday"), and your usuals with one-tap add. "What did you have at Worcester lunch?" opens a fast checklist sheet that ends in a single Log. Undo replaces every confirmation.

Benchmark
1 tap with history, 2 via usuals; about 5 on first run
Watch for
The win needs history. Check the first-run state and dragging the sheet down from inside the scrolled list.
Open F2
F3

Goal-driven plate builder

Set this meal's target on a tick ruler (defaults to what's left today) and get three real plates from open halls. Swipe a dish row to cycle alternatives; totals refit live. Hand-picked dishes stay locked, and "Fill to 850 cal" completes a plate around them.

Benchmark
5 taps plus one flick, about the same as today; the planner stays out of the way
Watch for
Plates are arithmetically right but sometimes odd pairings.
Open F3
F4

One surface, a physical tray

No push navigation in the core loop. Swipe between halls as pages, scrub meals on a time ruler, and jump stations with a thumb-height rail that replaces the right-edge scrubber. A maroon tray is always docked: tap to add, or long-press to lift a dish and throw it in. Pull the tray up to review and log.

Benchmark
5 taps and 2 drags, no menu scrolling
Watch for
Four horizontal controls on one screen; long jumps on the rail need a flick. Text from the rail bleeds over the dish list.
